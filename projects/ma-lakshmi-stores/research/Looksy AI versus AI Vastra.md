# AI Vastra fits on paper, but unproven

Neither "Looksy AI" nor "AI Vastra" is, on the public record, a validated in-store saree try-on kiosk — both are young, essentially unreviewed e-commerce try-on widgets being asked to do a job neither has documented evidence of doing. Of the two, **AI Vastra is the clearly better-positioned candidate** for Ma Lakhsmi Stores: it is India-based, prices in INR, explicitly lists sarees/lehengas/bridal wear as supported garment categories, and is the only one of the two that names a "Retail Kiosk Solutions" product at all ([aivastra.com](https://aivastra.com)). Looksy AI, by contrast, is a Dubai- or San Francisco-registered web widget with no India presence, no saree/ethnic-wear mention anywhere in its marketing, and a dated, credible customer report of its app going fully unavailable ([Shopify Looksy reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/looksy/reviews)). But "better positioned" is a long way from "proven": both companies are unfunded, unreviewed by any Indian retail press, and — critically — neither publishes a shred of evidence (demo footage, case study, or independent review) that it actually renders saree drape, pleats, pallu, or a separately stitched blouse convincingly. AI Vastra's own site even runs an unsubstantiated "100% Design Accuracy" banner with no methodology behind it ([aivastra.com](https://aivastra.com)). The practical recommendation is to treat AI Vastra as the lead candidate to demo — not to buy — while keeping the ₹1.2–1.5 lakh/month-scale budget flexible enough to walk away if a live demo with Ma Lakhsmi's own saree stock doesn't hold up, and to keep AlphaBake (an Indian competitor with named, verifiable saree-retailer clients) in reserve as a benchmark ([Apparel Resources on AlphaBake](https://apparelresources.com/technology-news/retail-tech/alphabake-launches-virtual-try-technology-online-fashion-brands)).

## Both brand names are decoys for multiple, unrelated companies

Before any comparison can even start, the research surfaced a disambiguation problem that Soumyanil needs to resolve before contacting either vendor. "Looksy AI" resolves to at least three unconnected entities: a Shopify app listing with a San Francisco developer address, a UAE-registered company called **Looksy Tech FZCO** based in Dubai Silicon Oasis and founded by Kirill Pegov and Natalia Varga, and an unrelated consumer "Outfit Changer" photo app ([Looksy About Us](https://looksy.tech/about_us); [Shopify Looksy listing](https://apps.shopify.com/looksy); [hunted.space](https://hunted.space/product/looksy-ai-outfit-changer)). No source confirms whether the Dubai company and the Shopify-listed app are the same business. "AI Vastra" is less fragmented but still inconsistent: aivastra.com lists a Hyderabad corporate office and a Rajahmundry (Andhra Pradesh) head office and calls itself "a product by Nice Digitals," while the Shopify listing credits a different developer name, "Nice Interactive" (also Rajahmundry), and a recruiting listing for the same product describes it as Surat, Gujarat-based ([aivastra.com](https://aivastra.com); [Weekday job listing](https://jobs.weekday.works/product-marketing-executive-at-ai-vastra-wkdyhzl2g0)). None of this is necessarily disqualifying for a small vendor, but a buyer doing due diligence by brand name alone risks emailing or signing with the wrong entity. The first concrete step, regardless of which product wins on paper, is to get a single named legal entity, GST number, and signed quote in writing before any payment — not to assume the website and the Shopify listing are interchangeable.

## Pricing favors AI Vastra's INR, no-subscription model — but kiosk pricing is a blank

Both products are affordable at Ma Lakhsmi's revenue scale, but they're priced for fundamentally different things, and the one SKU that actually matters — the in-store tablet kiosk — has no public price from either vendor.

| | Looksy AI | AI Vastra |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Monthly USD subscription + credits (figures vary across pulls: $14.99–$29 entry, up to $79–$169 top tier) ([Shopify Looksy listing](https://apps.shopify.com/looksy)) | Prepaid, non-expiring INR credit packs, no subscription: ₹1,000 (160 try-ons) to ₹10,000 (2,000 try-ons) ([aivastra.com/pricing](https://aivastra.com/pricing)) |
| Currency | USD only, no INR option | INR-native, framed against Indian photoshoot costs |
| Saree category named | No | Yes — sarees, lehengas, bridal wear explicit |
| Kiosk/in-store SKU | Not found; site funnels custom volume to a sales quote | Named ("Retail Kiosk Solutions") but **price, hardware spec, and per-device licensing are undisclosed anywhere public** ([aivastra.com](https://aivastra.com)) |
| Device/store cap | None stated | None stated |

AI Vastra's "pay only for successful try-ons," no-expiry credit model suits a store that doesn't yet have steady walk-in volume better than Looksy's recurring monthly fee, and its Starter pack is explicitly labeled "Perfect for Startups/Small Businesses" ([aivastra.com/pricing](https://aivastra.com/pricing)). Both are trivial costs against a ₹1.2–1.5 lakh/month revenue base even at the top tier. The real budget risk is that the only product line relevant to the Premium Lounge build-out — the kiosk — is sold off-menu by both vendors, via a "contact us"/demo-request flow, which means the actual number Soumyanil will be quoted could range from a modest license fee to a hardware-bundled enterprise deal, and there's no public data to anchor an expectation either way.

## Neither vendor has shown it can drape a saree, let alone a blouse

This is the dimension that matters most for a "Premium Lounge" positioned around status-conscious, trend-literal customers, and it's also the dimension with the thinnest evidence on both sides. AI Vastra claims its model is "specifically trained on Indian garment types," lists sarees/lehengas/salwar kameez/sherwanis/bridal wear ahead of Western categories, and says it "understands how traditional fabrics fall on a body" — but the words "pleats" and "pallu" appear nowhere on its own site, and its "100% Design Accuracy" claim carries no methodology or sample size ([aivastra.com](https://aivastra.com)). Looksy, across all three entities trading under that name, never mentions India, sarees, or draped garments at all — its marketing describes only generic Western "clothing, eyewear, jewelry, and bags," which strongly suggests its underlying model was trained and validated on stitched, seamed garments rather than a 5.5-meter unstitched draped fabric ([Looksy About Us](https://looksy.tech/about_us)). Blouse try-on — arguably the single biggest in-store customization decision a saree buyer makes, since blouses are usually stitched separately — is not mentioned as a distinct feature by either vendor; for comparison, Meesho's internal Google Cloud-built tool treats blouse, saree body, and pallu as three separate image inputs, showing this is a solved problem elsewhere in the Indian saree-tech space even though neither Looksy nor AI Vastra has demonstrated it ([Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/virtual-try-on-technology-with-google-cloud-ai)). No demo video, screenshot, or independent review of saree-specific rendering quality exists in public for either product — AI Vastra's "Trusted by" banner names Myntra, Mirraa, and Dalmia, but with no attributed quote, no saree-specific context, and zero corroboration elsewhere online ([aivastra.com](https://aivastra.com)). Both vendors offer a free live demo; given the total absence of saree-specific proof, a demo run against Ma Lakhsmi's own stock — specifically testing pleat fall, pallu drape, and blouse handling — is not optional due diligence, it is the only way to find out if either product works for this use case at all.

## Hardware is standard, but neither is a built kiosk product and neither touches Marg ERP or Zoho Inventory

Both products, as documented, are browser-based photo-upload widgets designed for e-commerce product pages, not purpose-built kiosk hardware — no vendor-specified tablet spec, camera rig, or depth sensor was found for either, which in practice means a mid-range Android or iPad tablet with a decent camera and stable Wi-Fi is the realistic floor, not a special device ([About Looksy](https://looksy.tech/about_us); [Ai Vastra: Virtual Try-On – Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/aivastra)). This is in contrast to other Indian in-store try-on vendors — Trupik's 3D body-scan cameras, Van Heusen's Style Studio floor-to-ceiling mirror scanner, PointAI's near-instant in-store system with Aditya Birla Fashion and Retail — which do use dedicated scanning hardware and have visible deployments; neither Looksy nor AI Vastra has shown anything comparable ([Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/technology-news/retail-tech/ai-turns-visual-merchandising-visual-experience-retail/)). Setup, where documented at all, assumes a live Shopify (or similar) storefront already exists: Looksy claims vendor-assisted, no-code onboarding within one business day for e-commerce sites, while AI Vastra's listing is a standard Shopify app install with no stated timeline ([About Looksy](https://looksy.tech/about_us); [Ai Vastra: Virtual Try-On – Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/aivastra)). Neither has any documented path for a tablet kiosk that isn't simply a browser pointed at that storefront. More importantly for Ma Lakhsmi's current priorities, **neither product integrates with Marg ERP, Zoho Inventory, Tally, or any POS/inventory system** — both read and write only an e-commerce catalog (Shopify products/collections) ([Ai Vastra: Virtual Try-On – Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/aivastra)). That means deploying either tool does nothing to advance, and is entirely decoupled from, current priority #1 (deploying the POS/inventory system) — stock tagged by fabric, trend, and origin in Marg ERP or Zoho Inventory would have to be mirrored manually into whatever catalog feeds the try-on widget, an ongoing sync burden neither vendor's documentation addresses. Both tools also appear to require constant internet connectivity with no offline mode, since processing is server-side and billing is credit-metered — worth a 4G/5G failover plan given Jalpaiguri's Tier-2 connectivity profile ([Looksy — About Us](https://looksy.tech/about_us)).

## India support exists for one vendor, but "exists" is a low bar

AI Vastra lists an Indian mobile support number and physical Indian office addresses, which at minimum makes it reachable in a way Looksy — whose only listed contacts are a UAE phone/WhatsApp number or a US-based in-app chat with no email or phone — is not ([aivastra.com](https://aivastra.com); [looksy.tech/about_us](https://looksy.tech/about_us)). But reachability is not the same as a track record. AI Vastra has **zero independent reviews anywhere** — no Shopify ratings, no G2/Capterra/Trustpilot listings, no press coverage under either "AI Vastra" or "Nice Digitals" — meaning there is no third-party evidence of its onboarding speed, support responsiveness, or reliability, good or bad ([Shopify App Store: AI Vastra](https://apps.shopify.com/aivastra)). Looksy has a thin but real review history — 3.7/5 from 9 reviews, bimodal (6 five-star, 1 three-star, 1 two-star, 1 one-star) — and one of those reviews, dated October 6, 2026, reports the app going fully "unavailable" to a live UK merchant, with the reviewer asking outright whether it had shut down without notice; a separate aggregator lists another complaint about the app "frequently disconnecting" ([Shopify Looksy reviews](https://apps.shopify.com/looksy/reviews); [AppNavigator: Looksy](https://appnavigator.io/app/looksy/reviews/)). Neither company shows any disclosed funding, headcount, or multi-year operating history — Looksy's Shopify-listed app launched December 2025, AI Vastra's launched August 2026 with zero reviews to date — so both should be treated as small, early-stage, and not insulated by investor or press scrutiny, a real risk for a store planning around a six-month hardware and software rollout through April 2027.

## Conclusion

The honest state of this comparison is that it is not really AI Vastra versus Looksy on saree try-on quality — it is AI Vastra's *positioning* versus Looksy's *irrelevance*, with neither vendor having actually proven the thing Ma Lakhsmi Stores needs proven: that a camera-and-screen kiosk can convincingly show a customer how a specific saree will drape, pleat, and pair with a blouse on her own body. Given that, the right move is not "buy AI Vastra" but "demo AI Vastra against real store stock before spending a rupee on hardware," while treating the undisclosed kiosk pricing and the complete absence of a verified Indian saree-retail reference customer as deal-breakers if they can't be resolved in a sales call. The **main risk of choosing AI Vastra** is that its saree-specific accuracy claims — including the unsupported "100% Design Accuracy" banner — are entirely unverified marketing copy from a company with no public reviews, no disclosed funding, and conflicting statements about its own headquarters; Soumyanil could commit the Premium Lounge build-out to a vendor that turns out to render pleats and pallu no better than a generic Western-garment tool, or that goes quiet the way Looksy's own customer reported happening to it. Given the six-month runway to April 2027, the lowest-risk sequencing is to pilot AI Vastra's cheap, no-subscription AI Catalogue product first (useful for the Instagram/WhatsApp launch funnel regardless of kiosk decisions, at ₹1,000–₹2,500 entry), use that engagement to extract a real kiosk quote and a live saree demo, and keep AlphaBake — which has named, quotable Indian saree-retailer clients including Jade Blue and Prashanti Sarees — as the benchmark to walk toward if AI Vastra's live demo doesn't convince ([Apparel Resources on AlphaBake](https://apparelresources.com/technology-news/retail-tech/alphabake-launches-virtual-try-technology-online-fashion-brands)).
