# Ma Lakhsmi Stores — Local Saree Sellers Competitor Analysis

**Scope:** Local/regional direct competitors — Jalpaiguri town + Siliguri (North Bengal)
**Competitors researched:** National Textile (Jalpaiguri), Td and Sons (Jalpaiguri), Avantra by Trends (Siliguri)
**Date:** 2026-10-24

## Summary

This first pass surfaced a split market: two **data-poor, digitally invisible Din Bazar incumbents** in Jalpaiguri town, and one **national chain** (Avantra by Trends, owned by Reliance Retail) operating a Siliguri franchise with real but dated review traction. The gap between them is the story — there is currently no local player occupying the "curated, trend-forward, digitally-discoverable" middle ground Ma Lakhsmi Stores is designed to fill.

## National Textile (Jalpaiguri, Din Bazar Cloth Market)

**Confidence: low — directory-only.** The only independent source found is a single self-submitted B2B trade-directory listing on textileinfomedia.com (retrieved 2026-10-24); no website, no Google Maps/Justdial presence, no social media, no customer reviews turned up anywhere despite multiple targeted searches.

Self-reported: a proprietorship on Dinbazar Rd with **₹50 lakh–1 crore annual turnover and 26–50 staff** — figures that, if accurate, imply a real, established operation, but these are unverified directory claims, not audited numbers. It operates as **both wholesaler and retailer**, which likely gives it direct sourcing relationships and bulk-pricing leverage beyond a pure retail counter. Catalog spans the generic full range (banarasi, brocade, chanderi, cotton, georgette, silk, synthetic, designer, handloom, kota doria) with **no stated trend/aesthetic positioning** — a legacy wholesale-retail hybrid, not a curated brand.

**What's working:** its Din Bazar location is itself a traffic/trust signal — the traditional cloth-trading hub where price-conscious local buyers already go by habit.

**What's not working:** a complete absence of any digital footprint (no website, no social, no reviews) — precisely the invisibility Ma Lakhsmi Stores' Instagram/WhatsApp funnel is built to exploit.

**Watch next:** whether National Textile (or Din Bazar sellers generally) ever shows up on Instagram/WhatsApp — right now that absence is the single biggest opening for Ma Lakhsmi's positioning.

## Td and Sons (Jalpaiguri, Din Bazar Cloth Market)

**Confidence: low — directory-only**, same single-source caveat as above (textileinfomedia.com, retrieved 2026-10-24).

Self-reported 26–50 employees against a listed **≤₹10 lakh annual turnover** — an internally inconsistent pair of figures, worth treating with real skepticism. Same Din Bazar cluster, same generic full-range saree catalog, same **wholesaler + retailer hybrid structure**. Listed payment methods (cheque/bank transfer only, no UPI/cards mentioned) suggest either stale directory data or a trade-counter operation not optimized for modern retail convenience.

**What's working:** embedded foot traffic from being inside the established Din Bazar wholesale ecosystem (inference, not independently sourced).

**What's not working:** zero discoverable online presence, zero reviews anywhere, and self-reported numbers that don't internally add up.

**Watch next:** same signal as National Textile — the moment any Din Bazar incumbent goes digital is the moment this gap starts closing.

## Avantra by Trends (City Centre Mall, Siliguri)

**Confidence: moderate** — this is a real, well-documented brand with trade-press coverage, but **site-specific data for the Siliguri franchise is thin and largely dated (2022–23).**

This is **not an independent local competitor** — it's a national saree/ethnic-wear chain launched by **Reliance Retail** in Sept 2021, reaching 73 stores across 9+ states by May 2024 (source: [IndiaRetailing, May 2024](https://www.indiaretailing.com/2024/05/20/reliance-retails-avantra-by-trends-opens-73rd-store-pan-india)) at roughly 2 stores/month in 2023–24. The Siliguri outlet (opened by Feb 2023, per [FashionNetwork](https://in.fashionnetwork.com/news/Avantra-by-Trends-opens-seventh-store-in-Bengaluru,1485661.html)) is a **franchise**, so it's locally operated but backed by Reliance's national supply chain.

**Positioning:** "a house of India's finest ethnic wear" — runs **10 in-house sub-brands** segmenting price/occasion tiers (e.g. Rimaya for premium pure silk, Vivitka for affordable art-silk), plus collabs with Nalli Silks and Pothys and direct weaver-cluster partnerships — a national-scale version of Ma Lakhsmi's own "bypass middlemen, source direct" pitch. Omnichannel: shop-in-shop + standalone stores + online via `avantra.ajio.com`.

**What's working:** a **4.5/5 average from 42 reviews** on a Siliguri listing site ([Yappe](https://yappe.in/west-bengal/siliguri/avantra-by-trends/861196)) — though the visible review text is ~3.5 years old (2022–23 vintage) and no fresh 2024–2026 reviews were found for this specific location, so treat the headline rating as a **legacy signal, not current-state proof**. Positive reviews cite variety across price points and helpful staff; mall-anchor footfall gives a "modern/trustworthy" retail association that independent Instagram sellers lack.

**What's not working:** one unverified negative review alleges billing overcharges vs. displayed prices and no shopping bags provided — an anecdotal but concrete execution/trust gap. More structurally: **no evidence anywhere of AI try-on, WhatsApp-native marketing, or Instagram-led local funnel** — Avantra leans on mall footfall and brand pull, not the hyper-local digital funnel Ma Lakhsmi is planning. Being Reliance-owned also means weaker personal/founder-led local trust than an owner-operated store can offer.

**Watch next:** whether Avantra starts running localized Instagram/WhatsApp marketing in Siliguri — right now its digital-engagement gap is real, but its inventory breadth and buying power are a scale advantage no local player (including Ma Lakhsmi, at launch) can match.

## What this means for Ma Lakhsmi Stores

The two Jalpaiguri incumbents aren't really competing on the same axis Ma Lakhsmi is — they're invisible online and undifferentiated in positioning, which is exactly the gap the business plan is built to exploit. The real structural lesson is from Avantra: **direct sourcing and weaver-cluster partnerships at scale is the one thing that shows up repeatedly as a real competitive asset** (both Din Bazar incumbents and Avantra lean on some version of direct/bulk sourcing), while Avantra's other advantages — sub-brand segmentation, omnichannel e-commerce, franchise-fueled expansion — are differentiators none of the small local players have, and none of them are matched by a hyper-local digital funnel or AI try-on experience either.
